# Lobby redesign: Blender feasibility

This is a concept and implementation plan, not an installed revision. It preserves the long transit tunnel, six side gates, queue rooms and current shop, with a stronger sequence of tunnel architecture, projecting wayfinding and a far-end music stage. Geometry and light budgets below are **authoring targets, not measured performance**.

## Spatial proposal

**Tunnel kit.** Keep the existing 280-stud length and 70-stud nominal width. Author a 40-stud plain shell segment and a 40-stud segment with both side portals; use seven placements centered at Z −120/−80/−40/0/40/80/120. Portal variants at −80/0/80 keep each doorway's 20-stud opening. Keep the roof opaque and volumetric; service ribs follow the arch instead of forming isolated blocks. Separate low wall, curb and walkable collision from decorative roof/rib meshes. Cable trays, conduit bends, old junction boxes, brackets, gasket seams and bolt heads provide detail; seams and bolts mostly use a shared normal/roughness trim sheet.

**Wayfinding.** Keep each doorway's inward-facing lintel sign. Add a projecting, double-sided blade at each gate, around X ±24, Y14–16, with its readable faces toward ±Z. A 9 ×3.4-stud face shows `LEVEL 1` and a short directional arrow; status is smaller and secondary. This rotates the readable plane perpendicular to the long tunnel axis. Current ±X-facing signs become edge-on from an end-on approach, which the blade avoids. Test legibility from spawn, the center of all three gate rows and the opposite-end approach. Readability must survive the existing overhead welcome sign, arch ribs, shop frontage and stage towers. Depth-tested SurfaceGuis with `LightInfluence=0` suit the existing sign treatment; do not put distant labels through walls.

Keep the right-wall shop's entire Z−69.45 to−10.55 wall/approach strip unobstructed. Native focus entry bounds are X26.18–29.38, extending to X25.58–29.98 for a currently focused product; do not put cabinets, truss feet or sign supports there. Existing box captions, six-face artwork, light sources and invisible pressure plates stay intact. The shop's Source contains historical comments about the old box count; the actual `catalogue()` and current `ShopItemCount` determine the live row, so the redesign must not hard-code that comment's count.

**Furniture end caps.** Replace the cardboard-dominated visual mass with recognizable 90s institutional/mall furniture: worn patterned sofa sections, vinyl waiting benches, laminate desks, stackable chairs, steel filing cabinets, beige CRTs, photocopiers, wire stock trolleys and rolled carpet. Use 8–12 recognizable furniture families repeated in varied orientations, with a few large silhouette pieces. Near-end furniture stays behind the spawn/camera clearance. Far-end furniture becomes peripheral and rear storage around the stage, rather than a full-height foreground wall. Retain continuous end collision and an opaque backing: random gaps are visual, not escape routes. Furniture next to walking routes receives a few clean box colliders; tangled legs and cables are cosmetic.

**DJ end.** Reserve approximately Z110–128 for a raised deck about 28 studs wide, **4–5 studs high**, facing toward spawn. A modest stair/landing starts beyond the last gate's approach; verify its real bounds before installation. Put steel-truss speaker towers near X ±21–22/Z118, **floor/ledge mounted separately from the raised deck**, initially 22–23 studs tall and about 5 studs wide, with broad foot plates, visible crossed bracing, cable runs and stacked black loudspeakers. A 24-stud tower on the raised deck risks the curved roof: the nominal inner roof falls to roughly Y25 at X24, before rib clearance. Fit the tower's outer top corners, foot plates and speaker brackets against the real roof and ribs, not just its center point. The booth uses battered black flight cases, a twin CD/mixer deck, understated CRT display and a restrained cyan accent. Hanging line-array boxes and grille patterns are recognizable at player scale; model the silhouette and major handles, bake the perforation detail. Keep the entrance axis and the Z80 gate approaches open. Existing far barricade art cannot simply remain beneath this stage: foreground chair centers are Z125.6 and desk centers Z129.6, with collidable legs reaching the floor. The far invisible blocker begins around Z133.8, behind this proposed deck.

The party control currently sits on the right wall at X32.93/Z108 with E range 9 and click range 12. Preserve its identity, action and reachable footprint; the booth can visually explain the music without changing those interactions. Also preserve the enabled `SignalSweepPrompt` (range 10) on `TunnelDetails.ZyntraDispatchConcourse.SignalSweepButton` and the existing donation leaderboard/shop approach. Party mode is a timed light-color sequence, not observed music beat synchronization. Existing tunnel lamps are local Y29.45 with PointLight range24; the desired deck at Y4–5 remains 25.45–24.45 below them. Plan 2–4 small, shadowless local stage fill lights below the roof rather than assuming those lamps directly light the deck. Keep signs/readable control surfaces stable during party/sweep effects.

Blender fixture housings must leave an immediate **PointLight** on each real tunnel lens; `LobbyCeilingSweeps` specifically requires that class directly under its tagged BasePart. Do not replace the entire fixture with an emissive-only mesh. Keep the Level 3 bay's direct diffuser names `Lobby L3 Fluorescent Diffuser 1..3` and their `Lobby L3 Party Fluorescent` SurfaceLights; its fourth fitting is intentionally dead. Existing player music is SoundService-owned and non-positional, so stage speaker meshes remain cosmetic unless audio changes are separately requested.

## Asset budget and material reuse

Proposed starting targets for the **new cosmetic kit**, excluding unchanged bays, characters and shop:

| Kit | Unique assets and triangle target |
| --- | --- |
| Plain/portal shell, lower-wall/trim pieces | 4–6 assets, about 2,000–6,000 triangles each; seven shell placements |
| Full arch rib, service track/conduit kits | 4–6 assets; arch rib about 1,200–1,800 triangles reused at 14 rows |
| Projecting sign housing/mount | 1–2 assets, about 300–900 triangles each, six placements; text remains native UI |
| Furniture families | 8–12 assets, about 400–1,800 triangles each; roughly 25–45 strategically visible placements across both ends |
| Booth, deck, truss tower, speaker cabinet | 4–6 assets, about 800–3,000 triangles each; reuse towers/speakers |

Aim initially for roughly **120,000–180,000 instantiated triangles** for new cosmetics and about 30 or fewer unique MeshIds. These counts are a planning envelope to refine after the actual Blender audit; they are not a Roblox limit or a guarantee of device speed. Individual meshes stay comfortably below the currently documented 20,000-triangle ceiling. [Roblox general specifications](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/specifications)

Reuse existing concrete color IDs `137549469660951` / `111820708793535`, floor `134299652574760`, curb `135670497420182`, and the current cyan/aged-olive palette. Create a small metal/rubber trim sheet and one furniture atlas; use 512–1024 maps as needed and reserve larger maps for a visible hero surface. Bake surface detail into normal/roughness maps rather than modeling every screw. MeshPart PBR uses SurfaceAppearance maps. [Roblox PBR textures](https://create.roblox.com/docs/art/modeling/surface-appearance)

Upload each reusable mesh once, then clone the same mesh/material IDs in Studio. Split the asset library by reusable objects rather than importing every duplicated object in one giant scene. Use simple anchored collision proxies and disable unnecessary touch/query/collision on cosmetics. Retain the current 28 main lights and initially budget just 2–4 stage fill lights; decorative emissive strips need not each create a real light. Roblox documents shared-mesh instancing, texture reuse and simple collision as practical optimization measures. [Roblox performance guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/performance-optimization/improve)

3. Author/export the reusable kit and manifest of triangle counts, dimensions, MeshIds/material maps and authored collision proxies. Preserve launch/prompt/UI and audio contracts separately from art.
4. Only after review, parent performs fresh Studio baseline/CAS edits, full native backups, scoped export and bounded Play checks. Verify gate launches, shop/purchase approaches, party control, accessibility, reset/respawn and mobile/low-quality readability.
