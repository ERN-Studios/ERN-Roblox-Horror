# Backrooms Roblox artwork research and Stay Quiet gallery brief

Research date: **4 October 2026**. Target: **BACKROOMS: STAY QUIET [CO-OP HORROR]**, with a **1920 × 1080, 16:9** image gallery.

**Recommendation:** lead with two players hiding behind an office partition while the tall entity passes through a fluorescent doorway. Make the danger readable immediately, then use the gallery to reveal six recognizably different places. Combine the spatial clarity of **The Backrooms**, the staged player–monster relationships of **Escape The Backrooms**, and the distinctive branding of **DOORS**. Use **Unseen Liminality** as the most relevant newer market comparator. These are art-direction inferences, not claims of measured click-through uplift.

**Evidence and scope.** I visually inspected Roblox-delivered icons and public gallery images, using the public universe, game-information and thumbnail endpoints. “G1”, “G2”, etc. below identify the image order returned during this research, not a guaranteed personalized Home-page order. The reference register links to the exact images inspected and records their asset IDs. Icons were retrieved at 512 × 512 (1:1); gallery images at 768 × 432 (16:9). Those are delivered renditions, not proof of the original upload dimensions. This report describes current artwork, not a reconstructed 2025 campaign history. No gameplay, image-generation method or thumbnail conversion rate was verified.

The six Stay Quiet levels below come from the supplied brief; this research does not certify their current Studio appearance or mechanics. Production captures must use the authoritative live Studio assets. Only this Markdown report is a repository output; no Studio edits, publication, Git reconciliation or commits are part of this read-only research request.

## What the actual icons and galleries look like

All descriptions below are visual observations of the linked Roblox image renditions. Text amounts refer to artwork, excluding the website’s title and controls. Camera descriptions are visual estimates, not recovered camera settings. Design lessons are my interpretations.

### Apeirophobia

- **Square icon:** thin, branching black entity at the left of a dirty yellow office; a large white geometric emblem sits centrally/right. Murky olive shadows and film-like grain. No readable title word in this version. [Icon][ape-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** G1 peers around a wallpapered corner; G2 uses a low, tilted wide view of uneven office geometry. G3 reverses the restraint: black creature and player silhouette against saturated red, with a bright grin. G4–G9 show office, entity, courtyard, dark room, library-like space and indoor playground. Most are environmental wides, not face close-ups. [G1][ape-g1], [G3][ape-g3], [G5][ape-g5], [G9][ape-g9]
- **Text:** usually one white “Apeirophobia” word near a lower corner/edge; some small screenshot labels. The early yellow images include lettering within the scene. No paragraph selling points.
- **Lesson:** alternate environmental credibility with a clear threat image. Do not reproduce the darkest exposure; a thin black entity needs a lighter surface behind it.

### The Backrooms — TheInfernoBoy1

- **Square icon:** low viewpoint into olive-yellow office columns, fluorescent ceiling panels receding in depth; white “The Backrooms” lower left. No monster or player. [Icon][tb-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** G1 repeats that setting. G2 sits at table/chair height in a spectacularly colourful party room with balloons and foam ceiling tiles. G3 shows “DO YOU TRUST ME?” graffiti; G4 contrasts a dark foreground head silhouette with a pink room. Other views include concrete geometry, a long colonnade, courtyard, mall escalators, red corridor and sunny house. [G2][tb-g2], [G4][tb-g4], [G8][tb-g8]
- **Lighting/colour:** dingy yellow anchors the brand; candy colour, gray, red and daylight communicate variety. Usually eye-height or lower wide perspectives with strong vanishing lines.
- **Text:** consistent small two-word white title lower left; G1 adds platform symbols. Environmental writing is secondary.
- **Lesson:** make every level identifiable by architecture and palette. The party room is a particularly relevant reference for Stay Quiet’s mall level.

### Backrooms — Red Panda Industries

- **Square icon:** Dutch-angle yellow corridor, blooming ceiling lights and centered white, black-outlined pixel-style “BACKROOMS”. Environment first. [Icon][rp-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** G1 places an upright figure around a corner beside a black directional mark; G2 reveals the spindly entity beyond an empty wall. Other images show small players in broad yellow rooms, a blue industrial doorway, combat-like action, empty halls and paired avatars. Low viewpoints and long sightlines emphasize scale. [G1][rp-g1], [G2][rp-g2], [G8][rp-g8]
- **Lighting/colour:** much brighter yellow and stronger light bloom than Apeirophobia, with black or cyan interruptions.
- **Text:** G1–G4 carry “BACKROOMS” plus platform symbols along the bottom; G1 also says “UPDATE!” upper right. Later images are text-free.
- **Lesson:** familiar yellow geometry instantly identifies the genre, but repeated similar corridors make a weak six-level showcase. Preserve variety rather than treating every frame as another yellow chase.

### Shrek in The Backrooms — The MonkeyMan Fan Club

- **Square icon and G1:** enormous frontal green Shrek face, bright yellow office behind, heavy white “BACKROOMS” with black outline across the top. One word, very large. [Icon][shrek-icon], [G1][shrek-g1]
- **16:9 gallery:** G2 is a grid of level previews under “Explore 30+ Levels!”; other frames show exaggerated characters, a black grinning creature lunging toward a low camera, and a hazmat player reaching a ladder while pursued. G8 is a frontal character lineup. [G2][shrek-g2], [G4][shrek-g4], [G5][shrek-g5]
- **Lighting/colour:** mostly bright, saturated and legible; strong green/yellow/black separation. Chase images use diagonals and large foreground silhouettes, often with no added text.
- **Lesson:** an instantly readable threat–escape relationship is valuable. Borrow the silhouette hierarchy, not its third-party characters or comedy tone. Its small level collage demonstrates breadth but sacrifices individual scene readability on a phone.

### Backrooms Morphs 2 — 2026 entrant

- **Square icon:** mint-green cartoon letter-like character on a gray radial background, topped by yellow “NEW UPDATE!” with thick black/white edging. Product/update announcement rather than atmospheric horror. [Icon][morph-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** G1 puts a small avatar and collectible prompt beside a huge dog-like head, with “RUN!”, a red arrow and hand cursor. G2 shows a classic noob avatar in an orange-ceilinged theatre room, labeled “Theater area”. G3 shows an avatar between red and blue slide openings under “Which slide?”. [G1][morph-g1], [G2][morph-g2], [G3][morph-g3]
- **Camera/colour/text:** frontal or modest three-quarter views, bright flat readability, yellow offices, primary colours. One- or two-word headlines are visually dominant; G1 also has UI-like text.
- **Lesson:** make the interaction understandable. For Stay Quiet, pose players hiding or crossing a gap; omit reward prompts, arrows and toy-collection cues that would promise a different game.

### Escape The Backrooms — Dervex, 2026 entrant

- **Square icon:** a yellow hazmat avatar pressed against a near wall on the right; elongated black entity occupies the corridor on the left. Diagonal motion streaks increase urgency. No title overlay. [Icon][escape-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** G1 observes two hazmat players from behind, aiming light toward a distant entity. G2 places player and entity on opposite sides of floor pits. G3 uses a foreground player to scale a tree-like threat. G4 stages a corner reveal; G5 has two players running toward the viewer, entity behind. [G1][escape-g1], [G2][escape-g2], [G5][escape-g5]
- **Lighting/colour/text:** yellow/olive offices, bright fluorescent fixtures, black creatures, directional flashlight haze; no added headline or logo across the five images. Medium/wide cameras preserve the player–threat relationship.
- **Lesson:** co-op can be communicated without the words “co-op”. This is an especially useful staging reference, although its small audience does not prove the artwork performs well.

### Unseen Liminality — 2026 breakout comparator

- **Square icon:** tilted collage of office and mall spaces with a large chalk-like spiral-eye symbol across the centre, white frame accents, no title words. [Icon][unseen-icon]
- **16:9 promotional images:** G1 has a worried blocky avatar at a wall with a tall silhouette through a doorway; G2 layers an avatar over huge condensed title lettering; G3 places an imposing figure in a mall; G4 combines a dark avatar, join-notification graphic and repeated “TRUST NO ONE”. White tall lettering, chromatic fringes and skewed borders create an analog-horror identity. [G1][unseen-g1], [G3][unseen-g3], [G4][unseen-g4]
- **Gallery continuation:** G5–G10 switch to clean, text-free architectural views: yellow office, brown corridors, furniture showroom, barred interior and blue poolrooms. Eye-height wides and long perspectives dominate. [G7][unseen-g7], [G8][unseen-g8]
- **Lesson:** separate attention-grabbing promotional frames from atmospheric location proof. Its text ranges from zero to large branding plus repeated phrases; there is no single “all successful horror thumbnails use no text” formula.

### Interliminality

- **Square icon:** purple, dark rounded face with stark glowing white eyes, orange flame along the upper edge, jagged white title near the bottom. A character-led badge. [Icon][inter-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** mostly very dark empty interiors: shuttered passage, reception/office spaces, courtyard windows, stairwell and purple-lit corridors. Cameras are mainly eye-height wides; the staircase is seen from above. Sparse pools of light carry the composition rather than visible players. [G2][inter-g2], [G7][inter-g7], [G8][inter-g8]

- **Text:** one angular “INTERLIMINALITY” logo, small lower right in the early images and larger bottom-centred later. A reception wall also contains in-world office signage.
- **Lesson:** a gallery can sell uneasy emptiness. For Stay Quiet, use at most one similarly restrained frame; the darkest images conceal too much architecture when reduced to discovery-card size.

### THE BACKROOMS [REDACTED]

- **Square icon:** evenly lit yellow office, central white pixel-style “THE BACKROOMS”, red “[REDACTED]” and small “SURVIVAL”. Environment rather than entity. [Icon][redacted-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** furniture pile in an office, lavender palm landscape, purple-lit fantasy terrain, overhead view of a tiled room, dark hotel corridor, library, ornate lobby, blue indoor pool and deliberately retro computer-error environment. - **Text:** repeated compact bottom-centred title and bracketed subtitle; limited incidental environmental/UI text.
- **Lesson:** visual diversity does not require a different monster in every picture. Useful precedent for pastel voids and poolrooms, but a Stay Quiet lead image should explain the co-op danger more directly.

### DOORS

- **Square icon:** green-framed doorway with a creature leaning out, a bright eye and small adjacent props; the stylized “DOORS” wordmark dominates the lower portion. [Icon][doors-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** G1 repeats the green doorway encounter with smaller branding; G2 shows a group of dark, colourful-screen-headed figures. G3–G8 mostly place a very large white/cream wordmark on the left and a hotel room or doorway on the right. G5 uses red light behind a black one-eyed figure; G6 turns a similar doorway composition blue. [G1][doors-g1], [G5][doors-g5], [G6][doors-g6]
- **Camera/colour:** level medium/wide views and doorframe occlusion; warm wood/amber, red, blue and green variants. Strong silhouettes against illuminated openings.
- **Text:** generally one word, but often substantial image area. Sparse word count does not mean small branding.
- **Lesson:** establish a repeatable logo and recognizable doorway motif. Stay Quiet should retain more scene area initially because it lacks DOORS’ existing brand recognition.

### Pressure

- **Square icon:** centered, near-symmetrical black illustrated figure on vivid red; sharp white facial/hand highlights. No readable title in the icon. [Icon][pressure-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** red title/emblem art, black-and-white character illustration, electric-blue machinery, gray workstation, black figures on white, green monster close-up, glowing red cube, immense red-lit architecture and purple forms over water. This is a varied illustrated promotional collection, not simply nine matching gameplay screenshots. [G1][pressure-g1], [G3][pressure-g3], [G6][pressure-g6], [G8][pressure-g8]
- **Camera/colour:** frontal portrait, extreme close-up, angled machinery and low-angle scale shot. Several frames use almost a single bright hue against black.
- **Text:** “PRESSURE” appears in G1; most other frames have no added headline.
- **Lesson:** one strong colour field plus an unmistakable shape can carry a thumbnail. Give each Stay Quiet level a controlled dominant hue; avoid turning the whole gallery into unrelated poster styles.

### The Mimic

- **Square icon:** pale, damaged doll-like face in three-quarter portrait, black bobbed hair, gray halo and black background, with a tall white serif “THE MIMIC” logo along the bottom. [Icon][mimic-icon]
- **16:9 gallery:** G1 is a frontal muscular monster with arms spread and red vertical lights; G2 uses a monochrome creature with large central branding. - **Lighting/colour:** black, gray, crimson and amber; restrained background detail, directional face illumination and ember-like highlights. Character images are tighter than the Backrooms architectural galleries.
- **Text:** usually the two-word title, lower right or centrally enlarged; serif and rougher horror treatments occur within the set.
- **Lesson:** the porcelain doll can provide a memorable face for Level 6. Use uncanny stillness and material contrast, without copying damage, gore or another game’s character design.

## What is likely to earn a useful click

These are hypotheses derived from the inspected artwork, not causal conclusions from competitor visit counts. Large audiences may reflect years of updates, social reach, recommendation placement and gameplay quality.

| Visual decision | Specific observed precedent | Application to Stay Quiet |
|---|---|---|
| One readable danger and one readable response | Escape The Backrooms G1/G5; Shrek G5 | Players pressed behind cover while the entity passes; the viewer understands the situation without reading. |
| A silhouette against contrasting space | DOORS G5; Apeirophobia G5 | Place the tall dark entity in a lit office doorway, not against a black wall. |
| A place the viewer wants to enter | The Backrooms G2/G8; Unseen Liminality G7/G8 | Show the mall’s nostalgic party decor and the cinema’s distinctive architecture, with enough exposure to see them. |
| A single unsettling interruption | Unseen Liminality G1; The Backrooms G4 | One still figure or partially exposed threat inside a familiar scene. Leave a question, not visual confusion. |
| A strong colour identity | Pressure G3/G6; DOORS G5/G6 | Yellow office, cyan pools, peach/teal mall, violet cinema, pastel/black void, primary-colour playground. |
| Consistent, economical branding | DOORS gallery; The Backrooms gallery | One small repeatable title lockup; the composition supplies the rest. |
| Proof of variety after the lead image | Apeirophobia, The Backrooms, Unseen Liminality galleries | Show all six levels, not six reskins of an office chase. |

**The particular opportunity:** “stay quiet” implies anticipation, proximity and restraint. Many inspected character-led images show running toward the camera. A close, readable hiding moment can distinguish this game while still making the danger obvious. Do not imply microphone detection, voice-chat requirements, reviving, combat or other mechanics unless the current game actually supports them.

## Concrete art direction for the six-level gallery

**Deliverable plan:** eight 1920 × 1080 stills: one overall hero, one image per level, and one quiet environmental closing image. Also retain a text-free export of each. The eight-image gallery is a proposed sequence, not an upload performed by this research. If only six images are desired, use the six level images and make Level 1 the lead.

**Shared visual language:** cinematic Roblox scenes built from the actual game, with recognizable player proportions and original entities. One dominant action or question per image. Three depth planes: nearby cover/character, readable threat or route, then environment. Use restrained grain and practical light sources. Keep entity silhouettes sharper than background clutter. Let silence appear through body posture, hiding and empty space rather than giant sound-wave graphics.

**Composition targets — recommendations, not Roblox rules:** keep essential content roughly 5% inside the edges and out of the bottom 15%. Reserve a quieter upper-left or upper-right area for branding. The principal threat/player grouping should still read when the image is reduced to 320 × 180. The route, room type and threat should not require zooming. Avoid rigidly putting every monster in the centre.

**Logo:** use “BACKROOMS” as a small locator above the larger “STAY QUIET”. Off-white compact sans serif, with mild wear confined to edges. Place the lockup on the hero; use a smaller version consistently on level shots only if it survives mobile reduction. The full listing name remains **BACKROOMS: STAY QUIET [CO-OP HORROR]**; the bracketed category need not be burned into every image. Optional hero subline: “CO-OP HORROR”. No paragraph, feature checklist, fake chat popup or obligatory “NEW” badge.

**Copy budget:** proposed house style is **zero to four additional words**, beyond the logo, on any image. Prefer none on the six level shots. This is an art-direction choice, not an official Roblox word limit.

## Do and don’t

| Do | Don’t |
|---|---|
| Stage a specific relationship: hide, approach, wait, cross or escape. | Assemble unrelated monsters and players in a generic poster. |
| Give black entities a brighter wall, doorway or pool behind them. | Assume an almost-black picture communicates fear at mobile size. |
| Show two readable players in several frames to establish co-op. | Crowd every image with four or more tiny avatars. |
| Make the six environments visibly different. | Reuse yellow wallpaper and one running pose for every level. |
| Show the actual foam, porcelain, tile, fabric and plaster materials. | Invent a much more detailed monster or cinematic fidelity the game does not deliver. |
| Add atmosphere behind the important silhouettes. | Cover the subject with bloom, fog, motion blur, VHS glitches or noise. |
| Use original characters, brand marks and fictional cinema posters. | Copy Shrek, DOORS entities, Pressure illustrations, branded mascots or a competitor’s exact composition/logo. |
| Save clean versions for text and crop testing. | Bake a long subtitle and multiple update stickers into the only master. |
| Let the doll be uncanny through stillness, gaze and porcelain. | Use injured children, realistic wounds, gore or torture as the hook. |
| Verify depicted interactions against the live game before publishing art. | Advertise microphones hearing players, revives, weapons, rewards or player counts without verifying those features. |

## Roblox requirements versus design recommendations

**Image specifications.** Roblox recommends **16:9, ideally 1920 × 1080** thumbnails; other ratios are stretched. Detail pages support up to **10 images/videos**. Supported still formats include JPG, GIF, PNG, TGA and BMP. Its Home personalization instructions specify **under 3 MB** and **2–5 active thumbnails**. Keep crucial text away from the bottom, where metadata can cover it. For this project, deliver PNG masters and upload-ready PNG/JPG versions under 3 MB. The last choice is our production standard. [Roblox thumbnail documentation](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails).

**Icon specifications.** Use square artwork, at least **512 × 512**, and inspect at small sizes such as **150 × 150**. Roblox moderates uploaded icons. A square icon and a landscape thumbnail are different compositions. [Roblox icon documentation](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/experience-icons).

**Text limits.** The reviewed official image-thumbnail guidance states **no numeric maximum word count, character count or percentage of image occupied by text**. Do not present a “20% text rule” as Roblox policy. The documentation’s sparse-overlay and no-promotional-claims language appears specifically under **video** guidance. General accuracy and content standards still govern still-image text. Our zero-to-four-word overlay budget is a creative recommendation. [Roblox thumbnail documentation](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/publishing/thumbnails).

**Content and rights.** All artwork remains subject to moderation and Community Standards. These prohibit extreme gore/graphic violence, sexual content, profanity, intellectual-property infringement and deceptive or confusingly imitative metadata. Public-facing material for even **Restricted** experiences must be appropriate for all ages. A more mature experience label does not exempt its storefront imagery. For this brief, use non-graphic fictional threats; omit blood, wounds, child harm, fake rewards and misleading UI. Approval of a competitor’s image is not permission to reuse its content. [Community Standards](https://about.roblox.com/community-standards), [Restricted Content Policy](https://en.help.roblox.com/hc/en-us/articles/15869919570708-Restricted-Content-Policy).

**Accuracy and discovery.** Roblox recommends original, relevant imagery that represents the experience. Optimize for qualified play-through and satisfied players, not a misleading click that immediately bounces. [Roblox discovery guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/discovery).

## Production and evaluation handoff

1. **Capture from the live game.** Verify each level, entity and depicted interaction in Studio before staging. Use actual models and appropriate graphics settings; hide debug UI, player names and development overlays. Keep a record of the scene and capture settings. This report does not authorize or perform live changes.
2. **Make three genuinely different discovery candidates:** office hiding hero, porcelain-doll playground encounter, and pastel-void crossing. Each must make sense without having seen the other images. Keep the six-level gallery coherent separately.
3. **Inspect at 1920 × 1080, 320 × 180 and 160 × 90.** Ask an unfamiliar viewer to identify the setting, threat and player action after a brief glance. Check grayscale silhouette separation, bright-screen viewing and whether bottom metadata would hide something important. These preview sizes and review steps are our suggested checks.
4. **Use actual platform results.** Compare qualified play-through and subsequent engagement where available. Roblox’s discovery guidance emphasizes accurate metadata and relevant thumbnail variations. No universal win rate, minimum sample size or guaranteed improvement can be inferred from these competitor images. [Discovery guidance](https://create.roblox.com/docs/production/promotion/discovery).
5. **Deliver eight landscape masters and eight clean variants**, plus upload-ready exports, a separate square icon if commissioned, and concise alt text describing the real scene. Approval should check scene accuracy, readable small-size composition, licensed/original assets and consistent branding.

**First image to produce:** the office hiding hero. It communicates the title’s promise, Roblox co-op and the signature dark entity in one scene. **Next two:** the doll behind playground cover and the pastel void crossing; together they establish that Stay Quiet has a specific identity beyond the familiar yellow maze.
