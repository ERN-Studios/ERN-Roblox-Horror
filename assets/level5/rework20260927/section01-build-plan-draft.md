## Visual blockers in priority order

1. **Wrong canyon termination.** The current nearly flat blue modern-window grid fills the far view. The reference continues into many staggered pale residential shafts with offset open balconies, exterior zigzag stairs and scattered warm windows, fading into gray haze. Replace `DistantEndTower` in `BuildCourtyard`; do not reuse `modern = true` there.
2. **Wrong flanking facades.** The current left side is uninterrupted horizontal balcony bands down to the lawn; in the reference, a near cream residential wall with narrow dark stacked windows and a covered ground-level passage frames the left edge, while balcony tiers become prominent farther back. The current right tower also reads as uniform stripes; carve distinct balcony stacks and visible stair shafts behind the low houses. Build these as different facades, not two instances of the same `addBalconyTower` pattern.
3. **House fronts are repeated.** Five nearly equal sage gables currently line the right edge. The reference near house is larger in frame, has muted green horizontal siding, dark roof, thick white gable/eave trim, a deep porch on posts with white balustrade, divided windows and warm entry light. Recompose the near and middle fronts separately: vary width, roof pitch, roof color, porch depth, bay/window order, and setback; retain only two simplified distant houses. Avoid a universal four-opening `addClapboardHouse` layout for every unit.
4. **Landscape is too clean and straight.** The current pale path is broad and almost linear; mulch appears as isolated rectangles with round ball shrubs. Narrow the path into short tangent-joined bends, give it an irregular edge, and replace box beds with layered dark plants/low branches near the camera and smaller clusters farther away. The reference has a planted interior courtyard, not a sparse lawn strip.
5. **Ceiling/light/palette.** Current blue-gray regular grid is sharp and bright; reference ceiling is warm gray, very high, softened by atmospheric depth, with small scattered white lights and far fixtures fading. Keep the roof of the space closed. Reduce cool blue surfaces, add restrained warm lights at house entries and apartment landings; use localized haze/depth treatment that does not produce visible slabs or cover close details.

## Geometry sequence

1. In `BuildCourtyard`, keep the existing origin and gallery contract. Replace the far grid with **three staggered tower planes** at estimated z 135/175/210, different widths and heights, with deeper balcony voids and a few bridges/stair stacks that overlap in perspective. Leave no clear front-facing flat end wall in the primary view. Make distant tiers cheaper by using paired rail bands or thin mullions instead of full balusters.
2. Split the left edge into a near 2–3 story cream foreground facade, then a narrower vertical window shaft, then recessed balcony tiers. Model dark window openings as dark backing set behind frame/mullions; use cream piers to break the facade every 2–3 bays. Add the covered pale passage at ground level as actual opening and canopy.
3. On the right, keep towers behind houses. Give the visible nearest tower a 3D projecting balcony module: slab, front/side rail, door recess, warm landing light. Offset adjacent stacks in depth. Build alternating stair runs with **landings joined at their endpoints** and white guard rails; the present 3-part `RightStairRun` silhouettes are too isolated to read as circulation.
4. Replace house loop data with per-house frontage descriptors. Build the closest house first and inspect it in a portrait crop; target a distinct near porch with two main divided windows, front door, siding lines, roof shingles, white fascia/columns/railing and warm sconces. Give the next house a different gable/entry rhythm. Use simplified roofs and dark window blocks only for the smallest distant houses.
5. Shape the path with 8–12 contiguous segments or a low-poly mesh, preserving collision separately from decorative visible edges. Use overlapping wedge/triangular grass and mulch pieces with varied plant silhouettes. Put the heaviest planting at the right foreground and around the path bend, tapering density with distance.
6. Tune the camera **after the silhouette**: retain a human low eye (~5–6 studs), aim past the house row toward the staggered center towers, and iterate FOV/offset against a portrait 9:16 environment crop. Reference composition has a close cream wall at left, closest porch and planting at right, a curving path low in frame, and a tall ceiling over a deep hazy canyon. Do not treat the current 1677×1080 landscape capture as a pixel comparison to the portrait screenshot.
